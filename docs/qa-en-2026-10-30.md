# QA pass of the English edition, 30.10.2026: the course read as one book

All 154 lessons of `content/en/` were written on 27–30.10 by several translators in parallel, part by part. This pass checked them as one book, on the tree of `main` after the last part landed. It follows `docs/qa-2026-10-28.md`, which did the same for the Russian edition. What was checked, what was fixed, the numbers, and what is left.

Legal statements were not touched: `docs/legal-review.md` §8 is for the lawyer, and nothing in it was resolved here.

## 1. Consistency

**How.** Each term and spelling was searched across all 154 lessons, their metadata (quiz, cards, comic, guide, practice) and the shared diagrams, counting the variants and reading the places where they differed. Two things the Russian pass did by hand were done by script here: the contraction rate per lesson, and every number that is in the Russian lesson and not in the English one (the second found only localizations: rubles to dollars, 152-FZ to the GDPR, the Stack Overflow survey).

**One term for one thing.**

| Use | Not | Where it was |
|---|---|---|
| inquiry, inquiries | enquiry, enquiries | 557 places in 52 files (lessons and diagrams); 65 places already said inquiry. The ids, selectors and form paths in the practice (`#enquiry`, `/enquiry`) changed with them, in the starter, the solution, the checks and the diagrams that explain the form |
| check, checked, checkbox | tick, ticked, tick box | 150 places in 33 files. A pre-ticked box is a pre-checked one to an American reader, and `checked` is the attribute the lesson teaches |
| cart | basket | 13 places in four lessons (one “basket” is a woven one, in a story, and stays) |
| the brief | the project card (01/01 and 00/04) | the form is the brief, as the UI calls it |
| privacy notice | privacy policy | 50 places in 19 files. 10/06 and 15/08 each say once that Americans usually call it a privacy policy, and CalOPPA and the FTC keep the statute's word |
| `+1 000 000 0000` | `+1 555 000 0000` | the templates are made of zeros; nine places in five files had a 555 number |
| homemade | home-made | 8 places |
| ’ in concept titles | ' | 17 titles in `curriculum.yaml`, the only place the typographic rule was not checked |

The house-style test (`tests/unit/style-en.test.ts`) already held American spelling, sentence case and quotes for every lesson, and it was green; `og:locale` was the one place that said British: `en_GB` is now `en_US`.

**Voice.** Contractions per 1,000 words ran from 13.5 in part 10 to 3.2 in part 8, and ten lessons had under two per 1,000 words (06/08, 07/03, 07/06, 07/07, 08/02, 08/07, 04/09, 15/07, 15/11, 15/12: “it is”, “does not”, “you will”, next to Echo's comic line saying “don’t”). The negatives (do not, does not, cannot and the like) are contracted everywhere, outside bold, quotations and code; in the lessons where under 40% of the markers were contracted “it is”, “that is”, “there is”, “you are” and “you will” are too. 814 changes. Eleven contractions that mis-parse (“close to it is called JSON”, “What matters in it is the axes”, “some of it is still proposals”, “Behind it is a question”) were found by reading the diff and put back; the test is that the clause still parses when the contraction is read as two words.

**The example project.** Kate Alder, a land surveyor in Riverbend, is the same in every lesson: no second name, no second town, no street other than “0 Bank Street”, no price but zeros (the one `$40` is a tripwire in 11/10, and is the lesson's example, not Kate's).

**Facts and dates.** The years near fifteen names (Berners-Lee, Mosaic, Netscape, Let’s Encrypt, WordPress, Jamstack, GA4, WCAG 2.2 and others) were compared lesson by lesson, and the thresholds that more than one lesson states: 60 characters for a title, 50 to 160 for a description, 45 to 75 for a line, 4.5 to 1, 3 to 1, 44 by 44 and 24 by 24 pixels, 72 hours, 30 days. They agree. No contradiction was found, and none was invented: the certificate lifetimes of 01/07 and 09/03 (90 days now, 64 from 10 February 2027; 398, 200, 100 and 47 days by the CA/B Forum's dates) say the same thing in different words.

**Cross-references.** Every relative link of every English lesson resolves to a lesson that exists (there are no broken ones) and every link of the Russian lesson is there too, except one. Where the English text says “lesson N” or “part N”, N was compared with the Russian and with the course order: all agree. Two “lesson 7” in 08/04 meant the lesson on privacy by design in part 7 and read as lesson 7 of part 8 (the Jamstack lesson); they now name it and link it, and so do “lesson 2” and “lesson 3” beside them. Eight links the Russian has and the English did not were all in four law lessons that were rewritten for English readers. Seven are back where the sentence is still there (09/09 to 15/07 and 09/04; 15/07 to 09/09 and 15/11; 15/11 to 12/12 and 13/09; 15/13 to 14/07); the eighth, 15/13 to 07/07, became a link to 15/07, where the GDPR is taught.

**Repetition.** Pairs of lessons were compared by 8-word runs. The most any two share is 25 runs, which is a few sentences: FTC section 5 and the Endorsement Guides in 12/12 and 15/11, the national data protection authorities in 06/07 and 06/08. 15/11 is the later lesson and the consolidated one, so the earlier one is left, and each law lesson is dated and meant to stand alone. Nothing long was repeated.

## 2. The graph

- **Concepts.** 301, each taught exactly once, none by nobody; 404 requirements in 154 lessons, all resolved, none to a concept taught later in course order, no cycle. The English `teaches` and `requires` are the Russian ones (`content:check` holds it); the order was checked again from the English files.
- **Leaves.** The sixteen lessons nobody requires are the ones the Russian map has.
- **Titles on the map.** The map says “first: <the concepts it lacks>” on a locked lesson, with the titles joined by commas. 139 of 301 titles had a colon or a list inside them, so “first: table layout, font and the spacer GIF: what they were and why they went, semantic elements: header, nav, main…” ran together. They are noun phrases now (“Table layout and the spacer GIF”, “Semantic elements”, “Core Web Vitals”), none longer than seven words, with no colon and no inner comma except the three names that have one.
- **No Russian on English pages.** The head of every page (title, description, Open Graph, Twitter, JSON-LD), `en/llms.txt` and the visible text, `alt`, `title` and `aria-label` of every route, before and after pressing every control of every diagram, had no Cyrillic except what follows. The 156 English Open Graph cards are drawn from English titles (home, map and a long title were looked at). Four diagrams put a Russian word in front of an English answer (“Нет:”, “Правильно:”, “Не то: здесь”) or a Russian clause in the record the consent diagram shows: all four read in English now, and the Russian rendering is unchanged. Thirteen source titles were in Cyrillic; they are English glosses with “in Russian” (the URLs are percent-encoded, so no Cyrillic reaches the JSON-LD).
- **Deliberate Russian.** The Russian plural forms and the date in 04/10 (the lesson says what a language does to an interface); `.рф` in 01/04; and the optional Russian field of the practice in 13/02 and 14/07, which applies only when the learner's brief includes Russian.

## 3. Practice

Every practice step, all 8 English templates, in headless Chromium: the app's own `buildSrcdoc` over the rendered files, loaded into a `sandbox="allow-scripts"` iframe on the preview of the built site (so the app's CSP applies), the posted checks and the script errors read back. The runner is not committed (`.qa/`).

- **152 steps × 8 templates × 2 = 2,432 runs, twice** (before and after this pass's edits to the practice's ids and phone numbers).
- **Solution passes: 1,216 of 1,216. Starter fails: 1,216 of 1,216.** Every posted result has as many checks as the step declares. No script error, no page error.
- **Nothing was broken.** The English translators ran `tests/unit/content.test.ts` (jsdom) and it held; the browser agrees with it for every step, which the Russian pass could not say of 05/08.

## 5. Small known items

- **The Pixar story spine video** (12/05) is on the English page already: `How PIXAR Tells a Story: The Story Spine!` (Write for Animation), `Ay4OChxR4vQ`, whose oEmbed answers 200 today. The Russian page has no video, because a Russian one was never found. All 39 English videos in 35 lessons answer oEmbed 200, and their titles are the page's.
- **«Not yet translated» in the engine's English.** The edition is complete, so what promised more is gone: the `en/llms.txt` summary no longer says “being translated, part by part … so far” (it says “154 lessons in 17 parts”, and says “N of 154 … the rest still in Russian only” if a lesson is ever missing); the file list says “every lesson”, not “every translated lesson”; the page for a lesson without an English one (“hasn't been adapted for English-speaking readers yet”, not “is being written part by part”) and the map's tag (“in Russian only”) say nothing about the future. - **Tests that named a lesson as untranslated.** `edition.spec.ts` and `seo.spec.ts` used 02/01 as the lesson without an English page and failed once it had one; another session made them pick whichever lesson is untranslated and skip when there is none (14aa5a3), which is what they do today (a skipped test is five of the 126). ## 6. Links

- **The checker did not read the English sources.** `scripts/link-check.ts` loaded the Russian edition only, so the 300 URLs that only English lessons cite (EUR-Lex, eCFR, Cornell LII, legislation.gov.uk, the ASA, support.google.com, javascript.info and 48 more hosts) were never asked, in CI or here. It reads both editions now (`en/<lesson>` in its report): 1,007 URLs in all.
- **One was dead.** `pexels.com/license` answers 403 to a robot, the shape the Russian pass met in 15/10. It is the same replacement: Wikipedia's article on Pexels, whose license section states what the lesson relies on.
- **One was replaced before it failed.** `genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection` (16/03) is the host the Russian pass found answering 403 to Node's fetch on a runner; it is `owasp.org/www-community/attacks/PromptInjection` now, which the Russian lesson cites too.
- **One more answered a runner and not this machine.** The first Links run on the pushed commit found `help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/plans` dead (403) in 08/04, where it was fine from the workstation. Wikipedia's comparison of shopping cart software replaces it; the Shopify article on Wikipedia was already cited beside it. Of the 300 English-only URLs it was the only one the runner refused.
- **Three titles were in Cyrillic URLs**; they are percent-encoded, which is the same address.
- **The result.** A clean cache, this machine: `links: 1007 checked, 0 dead`. All 39 English videos answer oEmbed 200. A citation was replaced by an equally reputable one in both cases above, and none was dropped.
- **Runner-only failures** (a host that answers a runner differently from a workstation) cannot be seen from here; the Links workflow on the pushed commit is the check, and it found the one above.
